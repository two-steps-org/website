We turn down work when custom AI would be expensive theater. That is not a sales tactic — it is how we keep trust with teams who do have a real systems problem.

## Skip custom when

- **The data is not there** — and no one is funded to fix collection or labeling.
- **The process is undefined** — automating chaos scales chaos.
- **A commodity tool already wins** — and your differentiation is elsewhere.
- **Compliance cannot be met** — with the timeline or access you have today.

## What we recommend instead

Fix the workflow on paper. Buy the 80% solution. Run a two-week discovery that ends in a written blueprint — whether or not we build it.

## When custom is worth it

You have a repeatable decision, clear ownership, systems we can integrate with, and a metric that moves if we get it right. Then the build is an investment, not a bet.

Saying no early is how we make room for engagements that should exist — and how you avoid paying for a demo that never ships.
